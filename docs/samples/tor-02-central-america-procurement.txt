Términos de Referencia / Terms of Reference — Public Procurement Transparency and Anti-Corruption Assessment, Guatemala and Honduras

Background
A regional governance programme financed by a multilateral development bank supports the national procurement authorities of Guatemala and Honduras to strengthen integrity in public procurement. Both countries operate electronic procurement portals (Guatecompras and HonduCompras) that publish tenders and contract awards, but data quality is uneven, open contracting standards are only partly applied, and civil-society monitoring of contracts remains limited. Recent audit findings point to a high share of direct awards and emergency procurement in the health and roads sectors.

Objective
To assess the transparency and integrity of public procurement in both countries, identify corruption risks across the procurement cycle, and recommend reforms the procurement authorities and supreme audit institutions can take forward within the next two years.

Scope of work
- Map the legal and institutional framework for public procurement and anti-corruption in each country, including recent reforms and their implementation.
- Analyse three years of procurement data from the national portals to produce red-flag indicators (single bidding, direct awards, contract amendments, supplier concentration).
- Conduct interviews and focus groups with procurement officials, auditors, private-sector bidders and civil-society organizations.
- Assess mechanisms for citizen engagement and social audit of public contracts, including access to information requests and community monitoring of public works.
- Facilitate two national stakeholder workshops and one regional workshop to validate findings.
- Prepare policy analysis and a prioritized reform roadmap for each country.

Deliverables
1. Inception report and methodology.
2. Procurement data analysis with red-flag dashboard.
3. Country assessment reports for Guatemala and Honduras (in Spanish) with an English summary.
4. Regional synthesis and policy briefs.

Qualifications
- Minimum 8 years of experience in public procurement reform, anti-corruption or public financial management in Latin America.
- Demonstrated experience with procurement data analysis and open contracting.
- Experience designing or evaluating citizen engagement and social accountability mechanisms.
- Fluency in Spanish is required; working English.
- Experience in Central America strongly preferred; presence in Guatemala City or Tegucigalpa is an advantage.
- Firms or individual consultants may apply; teams should include a data analyst.
